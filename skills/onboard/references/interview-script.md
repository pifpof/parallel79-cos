# Onboarding interview script

The exact wording for every message the executive reads during `/onboard`. Adapt the wording to the person and their language, but keep the shape: one question, one why-line, example answers, three sentences or fewer.

Nine questions in two parts. Numbering runs 1 to 9 across both parts, so "We stopped at question 6" means the same thing everywhere.

---

## Message format

Every question message looks like this, and nothing more:

```
Question [n] of 9
[The question, one sentence.] [Why-line, one sentence.]
For example: [option] / [option] / [option]
```

The first question of each part opens with the part line instead of a bare number: `Part 1 of 2: how I work with you. Question 1 of 9.` The last question of a part adds `Last one in this part.`

Never add a second question, a preamble, or a recap of their previous answer, beyond the one-line acknowledgement where the script calls for it.

---

## The opening (sent before any file is read)

> This is our first meeting, like briefing a new chief of staff. Every answer becomes how I work with you and what I know about you.
>
> It's two short parts: first how you want me to work, then your world. About 9 questions, 15 to 20 minutes. Rough answers are fine, you can skip anything or change it later, and typing or dictating both work. If we've started before, I'll pick up where we left off.
>
> Please leave out passwords, account numbers and anything confidential to a company or board.
>
> Ready?

This is the one message allowed to run longer than three sentences. Send it as written, translated if the executive writes in another language.

---

## Part 1 of 2: how I work with you

### Question 1: day-to-day help
- **Ask:** "At a high level, what do you want help with day to day?"
- **Why:** "So I know what to keep an eye on for you."
- **Examples:** "staying on top of priorities / preparing for meetings / thinking through decisions"
- **If they answer with specific tasks:** park each one (see "Parking a specific task"), then name the high-level area in one line ("So: staying on top of news and your inbox.") and move to Question 2. Don't ask about the tasks.

### Question 2: doer or thinking partner
- **Ask:** "Do you mostly want a doer, a thinking partner who pushes back, or both?"
- **Why:** "So I know when to just get on with it and when to challenge you."
- **Examples:** "just get things done / push back when you see a gap / both, depending on the day"

### Question 3: information style
- **Ask:** "How do you like information?"
- **Why:** "So I write the way you read."
- **Examples:** "bottom line first, short / full story, detailed / something in between"
- **From the next message on**, every message follows this answer (see "Honouring the style answer").

### Question 4: name
- **Ask:** "What would you like to call me?"
- **Why:** "It's how I'll sign off and how you'll refer to me."
- **Examples:** "a name / just 'Chief of Staff' / decide later"
- "Later" is a complete answer. Use "Chief of Staff" until they choose.
- Add `Last one in this part.`

### Part 1 recap
> Here's what I've got: [help areas, in a few words]; [doer / thinking partner / both]; [information style]; call me [name]. Anything to change?

One line of content, then the question. If they change something, update it and move on without a second recap.

---

## Part 2 of 2: your world

### Question 5: who you are
- **Ask:** "Tell me who you are and what you do."
- **Why:** "So everything I do starts from your actual role."
- **Examples:** "your role and organisation / what the business does / how long you've been there"

### Question 6: priorities
- **Ask:** "What are the two or three things that matter most to you right now?"
- **Why:** "So I can keep pointing your time at what moves them."
- **Examples:** "a deal to close / a team to build / a launch, a board, a hire"
- One follow-up at most, only if the answer can't be written down as named priorities: "Which one matters most?"

### Question 7: typical week
- **Ask:** "What does a typical week look like?"
- **Why:** "So I know your rhythm and where I can save you time."
- **Examples:** "standing meetings / reports or reviews you prepare / the parts that eat your time"

### Question 8: key people
- **Ask:** "Who are the people I most need to know about?"
- **Why:** "So I recognise them when they come up."
- **Examples:** "your team / a board or investors / key clients or partners"
- First names and roles are enough. Don't ask for contact details.

### Question 9: anything that shapes everything
- **Ask:** "Is there anything about your situation right now that shapes everything else?"
- **Why:** "So I don't miss the context behind your decisions."
- **Examples:** "a big change underway / a deadline / nothing comes to mind"
- Add `Last one in this part.`

### Part 2 recap
> Here's what I've got: [role, organisation]; priorities: [1], [2], [3]; [one-line week]; [n] key people. Anything to change?

---

## Parking a specific task

When an answer describes a task to build ("a daily summary of my industry news from Gmail", "draft my board updates"), don't explore it. Reply in one line and return to the question:

> Saved for later: [the task, in their words]. [Next question, or back to the current one.]

Never ask about the task's format, schedule, sources or connections. Never design it. It goes on the "Things to set up later" list in `memory/profile/wishlist.md`.

---

## Checking what's already known

If you've picked up facts from past chats, use them only as a single confirmation question, in place of the scripted question it would answer:

> I've picked up that you sit on four boards. Is that right?

One fact per message. Never present a block of what you think you know. If unsure whether a fact is right, don't mention it; ask the scripted question instead.

---

## Honouring the style answer

Once Question 3 is answered, every following message matches it. For "short", that means the question and why-line only, examples cut to three words each. The brevity default is short regardless: three sentences or fewer, always.

---

## Resuming

If `memory/profile/onboarding-progress.md` shows onboarding in progress:

> We stopped at question [n]. Continue, or start over?

If it shows onboarding complete:

> You're already set up. Want to update one part? For example: how I work with you / who you are / priorities / your week / key people.

---

## The close

Send as one message, adapted to their style answer:

> We're set. Three habits make this work:
> 1. Start every conversation with me inside this project.
> 2. Talk to me like a colleague. No special commands needed.
> 3. Before you leave, type /save so I remember.
>
> To see what I know about you, open the memory → profile folder. Things we saved for later: [list]. A good one to try today: [one item, and the plain sentence they'd type to start it].

If nothing was parked, suggest one thing from their Question 1 answer instead.

---

## Words never to use with the executive

vault, CLAUDE.md, markdown, context window, frontmatter, skill, repository, index. Say "your assistant's files", "my instructions", "my memory", "the memory folder" instead.
